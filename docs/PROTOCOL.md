# Neewer MS60C BLE protocol (MAC-tagged frames)

Everything here was checked against three real **Neewer MS60C** lights, driven from Windows 11 with
[bleak](https://github.com/hbldh/bleak). Other Neewer models that use the same "Infinity" app protocol
probably work the same way, but that hasn't been tested. If you confirm another model, please open a PR.

## GATT

| | UUID |
|---|---|
| Service | `69400001-b5a3-f393-e0a9-e50e24dcca99` |
| Write (write-without-response) | `69400002-b5a3-f393-e0a9-e50e24dcca99` |
| Notify | `69400003-b5a3-f393-e0a9-e50e24dcca99` |

`tools/gatt_dump.py <MAC>` prints the full table for your own light.

## Frame

```
78 | tag | len | payload ... | checksum
```

- `78` is the fixed prefix.
- `len` is the number of payload bytes.
- `checksum` is the sum of **every byte before it**, prefix included, `& 0xFF`.
- On the MS60C, every command that does anything starts its payload with the **light's own MAC address**
  (6 bytes, in the order it is written), followed by a sub-tag and the values.

## Commands

| What | Frame | Values |
|---|---|---|
| Power | `78 8D 08 [MAC×6] 81 vv` | `01` = on, `02` = off |
| CCT | `78 90 0B [MAC×6] 87 bri kk gm 04` | bri 0–100 (%); kk = kelvin/100 (`38` = 5600 K); gm = green/magenta −50..+50, sent as value+50; `04` = dimming curve |
| HSI | `78 8F 0C [MAC×6] 86 hLo hHi sat bri 00` | hue 0–360 as a little-endian 16-bit value; sat and bri 0–100 |

Worked examples for MAC `AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:01`, generated by `neewer.py`:

```
power on    78 8d 08 aa bb cc dd ee 01 81 01 8c
power off   78 8d 08 aa bb cc dd ee 01 81 02 8d
5600K 80%   78 90 0b aa bb cc dd ee 01 87 50 38 32 04 55
blue 60%    78 8f 0c aa bb cc dd ee 01 86 f0 00 64 3c 00 26
```

After a power-on, the light needs about 150 ms before it takes a CCT or HSI frame (`neewer.py` waits that long between frames).

## The trap: the legacy commands

Older Neewer lights use short frames with no MAC, for example power `78 81 01 01 FB`, and most
reverse-engineering write-ups describe those. **The MS60C acks them on the notify characteristic but
does nothing with them.** If your light answers but doesn't react, this is why. `tools/probe.py <MAC>`
sends the legacy frames and prints what comes back on the notify characteristic, so you can see the ack.

The MAC-tagged format comes from the source of [NeewerLite](https://github.com/keefo/NeewerLite) (MIT),
a macOS app. Credit to its authors.

## Connection behaviour (observed)

- A fresh connect takes about 4 s with a strong signal, and up to about 30 s or a timeout when the signal is weak.
  From the PC used here, two of the lights sat at about −90 to −97 dBm.
- Holding the connection open (`neewerd.py`) brings each command down to about **100 ms**.
- A light that has lost power and come back **doesn't advertise until you press its power button**.
  Until then, scans won't find it.
- On Windows (WinRT), connecting while another scan is running tends to time out. `neewerd.py` runs one
  scan or connect at a time.

## Finding your lights

- `tools/scan_nw.py [seconds]` lists Neewer-looking advertisers, strongest signal first.
- `tools/color_ident.py MAC1 MAC2 MAC3` turns them red, green, blue and so on, so you can match each MAC to a light.
- `tools/blink.py MAC1 MAC2` makes the first light blink once, the second twice, and so on.
